## Summary

QWOM is real, published, and directly relevant — and Seth is personally credited in it. Full name: **Quickstart Wordlist of Melanesia**, SIL Language & Culture Archives entry 100200, issued 2024-07-24, authors Brendon E. Yoder, Steve Miller, Jonathan Moe; 154 pages; downloadable ONLY as .docx (267.71 KB) and .pdf (6.14 MB); content languages English + Indonesian + Tok Pisin; countries Indonesia, PNG, Solomon Islands, Vanuatu; publication status "Draft (posted 'as is' without peer review)". Its purpose is explicitly NOT lexicostatistics — it is to build a "core phonological database" for phonology → orthography → lexicography, which is exactly A-Z+T's job. Size is **">1,900 items"** (Miller 2024, DIU proceedings), with open-ended semantic-domain prompts intended to push the real yield past 2,000. Its lineage is CAWL → CPaWL (Comparative Papua New Guinea Wordlist, Moe 2023) → QWOM, with African items (elephant, hyena, tamarind, harvest season, honey) deliberately stripped. The **working spreadsheet is NOT publicly downloadable** — the archive page advertises a "digital version" with a PNG-Picture-Dictionary flag field and standardized audio filenames, but links no XLSX/CSV/LIFT; you must ask Steve Miller (steve_miller@sil.org) or Jonathan Moe. Licensing is the blocker: SIL's default Terms of Use are non-commercial-only and explicitly forbid mirroring "in part or whole" without written permission, so bundling QWOM inside GPL-3.0 A-Z+T needs written SIL consent. By sharp contrast, the SILCAWL LIFT that A-Z+T already auto-downloads is **MIT-licensed** (via WeSay), and I verified empirically that it contains **1,696 filled Indonesian glosses out of 1,700 entries** — an asset Seth's Indonesian-speaking colleagues could use today that nothing in the repo appears to advertise. On the code side, A-Z+T has a clean extension point (`WordListTemplate`/`CAWL` in backend/core/templates.py) but a hard `range(1700)` in tasks/chooser.py:302 and a hardcoded `ftype='SILCAWL'` in io_put/lift.py:4090 that would both have to become template-driven for a ~1,900-item QWOM. Finally, a significant and time-sensitive Dekereke finding: as of the **2026-08-22 Windows build, Dekereke does native LIFT import/export from its File menus** — superseding the older SFM/XML-only guidance still published on Casali's legacy page, and directly relevant to both azt and dekereke-pa-data-source.
